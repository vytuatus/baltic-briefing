Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Drone briefly enters Latvian airspace near the Russian border

Official statement Reuters and LSM from the Latvian armed forces; no attribution yet.

Latvia's armed forces said an unidentified drone entered Latvian airspace in the early hours of 27 November. An actual-threat alert was sent to phones in Ludza municipality shortly before 03:00 and lifted within minutes, followed by a lower-level alert for Balvi municipality that ended around 03:29. NATO fighters and an interceptor drone were deployed. At about the same time, the governor of Russia's Leningrad region said more than 40 drones had been downed in an attack on the region.

Critical take. Latvia has had several strays this year, some of them Ukrainian drones pushed off course by Russian jamming, and the timing points to the Leningrad attack. Until Latvia attributes it, this is an airspace incident, not evidence of a Russian probe.

Poland's foreign minister: downing missiles over Ukraine would be self-defence

Official statement Euromaidan Press (Ukrainian outlet) citing Polsat and RMF FM; Bloomberg's earlier report that Poland raised the idea at NATO still rests on unnamed sources.

Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said on Polish radio on 25 November that shooting down Russian missiles or drones over Ukraine at Kyiv's request would be necessary defence, because an approaching missile is a direct threat. Deputy Defence Minister Cezary Tomczyk said NATO is not in favour and hopes the planned no-fly zone along the eastern border, 12 to several dozen kilometres deep, will take effect in late December or early January. Ukraine's ambassador said Kyiv shares real-time missile and drone data with Poland.

Critical take. Sikorski's remarks make Poland's position clearer than Bloomberg's sourcing did, but there is still no NATO decision and Warsaw's concrete step remains a zone over its own territory.

Weekend GPS interference also hit Tallinn, Helsinki and Åland

Analysis Dagens Nyheter and DR via European Pravda; Yle on the Sparta II.

Dagens Nyheter and Danish analysis of gpsjam.org data, reported by European Pravda on 26 November, show that interference on 21–23 November was strongest around Stockholm but also affected Gotland, Tallinn, Helsinki and the Åland Islands; Tallinn and Helsinki still showed heavy interference on 23 November. Sweden's Transport Agency called it serious and found no natural cause, but no state was named. A Danish naval officer said suspicion falls on Russia. The same weekend a drone, which Ukraine's president said Ukraine sent, hit the Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland.

Critical take. A wider footprint for one episode, not a new episode. Some interference may be linked to Russian defences against Ukrainian drones and ship strikes, but nobody has shown that, and the effect on civil aviation is the same either way.

Latvia delays its Russia-border decision; Lithuania sees no need to close

Official statement Delfi via Bytes Europe and Glavnoe; LRT/BNS.

Latvia's prime minister, Andris Kulbergs, has asked the foreign and transport ministries to assess closing crossings with Russia, including the effect on transit, with the issue expected in the week of 30 November rather than on 26 November. Latvia's border guard chief has warned that traffic will shift to Latvia once Estonia closes its road crossings on 1 December. In Lithuania, new border guard chief Donatas Škarnulis told LRT there is no need to close the Russian crossings now, while President Gitanas Nausėda called Estonia's move appropriate and did not rule out similar steps.

Critical take. Three neighbours, three approaches. The disagreement is about timing and cost, and Russia's foreign ministry has already protested to Estonia, calling the closure an anti-Russian stunt.

Moscow: no plans to attack NATO, but NATO 'openly preparing' for war

Official statement TASS; Ukrainska Pravda on the protest to Estonia.

At her 20 November briefing, foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Russia has no intention of attacking NATO states and has proposed mutual security guarantees, while also saying NATO is openly preparing for war with Russia and noting that both sides have nuclear weapons. Three days later the ministry warned that Estonia was bringing the situation to the brink of open war, and on 23 November it protested Estonia's border closure.

Critical take. Reassurance and warning in the same week is a familiar pattern. Lithuania's threat assessment judges Russia by its rebuilding of military capacity, not by such statements; neither line changes that judgement.

Poland: false Kaliningrad narratives reached millions

Analysis GSC summary of Polish official estimates; Lithuanian and Polish fact-checks of the Kaliningrad claim earlier this week.

A public summary by the Global Security Centre, a Warsaw risk firm, cites Poland's state counter-disinformation centre as estimating that false narratives about the 21 November Kaliningrad alert reached at least 19 million people. It also notes that Russia's ambassador, summoned over the three Kh-101 missiles that fell in Poland on 20 November, said they were not Russian.

Critical take. Shows the reach of a story that had no official Russian backing. The audience figure is an estimate, and the firm's own risk ratings are its judgement.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified. Troop and logistics movements are only visible when someone publishes them, so a quiet indicator means nothing has been reported, not that nothing is happening.
