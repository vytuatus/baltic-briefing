Critical assessment

What the evidence supports, and where the coverage runs ahead of it.

Today's evidence remains consistent with the Lithuanian baseline: nothing points to Russian forces being freed from Ukraine or redirected toward the Baltic states, and the Baltic Fleet has just lost a missile ship to damage. The main development is on paper, not on the ground. Two major outlets now describe a written Russian position, dated 10 November and seen by The New York Times through a negotiator and confirmed by a second source, that would push NATO out of every state that joined after 1997, including all three Baltic states. That makes the broad demand, earlier reported only through anonymous expectations, much better supported, though the paper is unsigned, has not been published and the Kremlin has not commented on it. Bild's narrower claim that Moscow asked Finland to leave NATO is not in those accounts and stays contested. Coverage tends to call this an ultimatum, but it is an opening negotiating position that the US, Finland, the Baltic states and European leaders reject; its significance is that Moscow still frames peace in Ukraine as a rollback of Baltic security, as the baseline judges. Quiet logistics and medical indicators reflect a lack of public reporting, not proof that nothing is happening.

Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Leaked Russian paper: NATO back to 1997, no British or French nuclear umbrella for Europe

Imagery or documents The NYT says it obtained a copy from a negotiator and a second source confirmed it is authentic; The Telegraph's account matches. The paper itself has not been published, and the Kremlin has not commented.

The New York Times reported on 13 November that Russian officials circulated an unsigned 'non-paper' dated 10 November at the Abu Dhabi talks. According to the NYT and outlets summarising it, it asks NATO to pull troops and bases back to 1997 positions, give up further enlargement and any military activity in Ukraine, Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus and Central Asia, end Western arms supplies to Ukraine, keep Ukraine out of the EU's common defence policy, and asks Britain and France not to use their nuclear weapons to protect other European states. The Telegraph on 14 November described a paper with the same date and highlighted the demand on Britain's deterrent.

Critical take. Every Baltic state, Poland and Finland joined NATO after 1997, so this is a demand to remove allied forces from them. It is the most concrete evidence yet that Moscow links a Ukraine deal to rolling back Baltic security, as the Lithuanian assessment warns. It is still an opening position in talks, not an action.

US, Finnish and European leaders reject the 1997 demand

Official statement Ukrinform (Cotton and the US official, citing the NYT), Euronews (EPC declaration), Euromaidan Press (UK reaction).

US Senate intelligence committee chairman Tom Cotton called the idea of NATO members withdrawing from their own territory preposterous. A US official told reporters that the envoys reminded Moscow how much has changed since its 2021 demands. Finland's defence minister said the demands test US and European resolve. In Dublin, 43 European Political Community countries signed a declaration that European security is for Europeans to decide, and the UK prime minister said the same after the meeting.

Critical take. Western rejection is broad and on the record. Accounts differ on Europe's seat: Bloomberg, via ISW, says five countries plus an EU representative were named for technical talks, while Euronews reports no agreement yet on an EU envoy, and Russia has not said it accepts them.

Estonia: Russian missile ship hit several times, with dead and wounded

Official statement ERR, Kyiv Post and others report the same briefing; Ukraine's SBU made the original claim; Russia has still not commented.

At a defence ministry briefing on 13 November, an Estonian Defence Forces colonel said more than three Ukrainian drones hit the Baltic Fleet corvette Naro-Fominsk on 11 November, causing deaths and injuries, and that it could not continue under its own power and was escorted toward St Petersburg. He said the same night's strike on Ust-Luga cut the port's oil throughput from about 700,000 to about 300,000 tonnes a day, and that a Russian helicopter was destroyed by its own air defence.

Critical take. An independent NATO-country military now confirms the strike, so the claim moves to confirmed. The Baltic Fleet is losing ships and attention to Ukrainian attacks, which is the opposite of forces being freed for the Baltic direction.

Moscow and Minsk: NATO is the threat, we are not preparing for war

Official statement Russian and Belarusian state media; no independent reporting needed for the fact that they said it.

Foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova told TASS on 14 November that NATO undermines Europe's security like 'a bad tooth' and that Europe would be safer without it, replying to Germany's chancellor. On 11 November Belarusian state TV quoted Lukashenko telling a CSTO meeting that Belarus and Russia are not preparing for war with NATO or the EU by 2030 and accusing Europe of militarising.

Critical take. Both lines serve the same goal as the leaked paper: to present NATO's eastern presence as the problem. Denials of intent cannot be tested; the baseline judges that Russia is rebuilding capacity for possible limited operations in the longer term, regardless of what it says now.

Poland plans to extend its closed zone on the Belarus border

Official statement Defence24 reporting on the draft regulation.

Poland's interior ministry has drafted a regulation to extend the temporary ban on entering a strip along 78 km of the Belarus border for 90 days, from 30 November to 27 February 2027. It counts 482 attempted illegal crossings this year up to 8 November, calls the migration pressure artificially created by Belarus, and notes more balloons and drones from Belarus, mostly for smuggling, without ruling out destabilisation.

Critical take. A steady, managed pressure rather than a new escalation. It matches the Baltic experience with balloons from Belarus and the baseline's description of hybrid pressure from Minsk.

Commentators claim Ukraine is deliberately routing drones through NATO airspace

Commentary No official, investigative or imagery-based source supports deliberate routing or the balloon claim.

Pro-Kremlin and contrarian commentators, including a widely read Substack blog, argue that Ukraine deliberately sends drones through Polish and Baltic airspace to avoid Russian air defences, and repeat an unverified claim that a balloon from the Baltics delivered a bomb used in an attack in St Petersburg. Estonia's foreign minister says no state has been authorised to fly drones through Estonian airspace; Estonia's defence minister says most drones stayed in Russian airspace.

Critical take. Stray drones, likely knocked off course by Russian electronic warfare according to Lithuania's defence minister, are a real safety problem, but intent has not been shown. The narrative helps Moscow cast the Baltic states as co-belligerents.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified.
