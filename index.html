Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Estonia pushes back on reported Pentagon request to soften its tone

Official statement, on the record Estonian statements via Delfi, ERR and LSM; Reuters remains the only source for the request itself.

After Reuters reported that Pentagon officials asked the Baltic states to tone down warnings about Russia, Estonian PM Kristen Michal told Delfi he would not hold back and called Putin a dictator who kills women and children. Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said the US made no official proposal; ministry secretary general Jonatan Vseviov said he knew of no official request. Estonia's defence ministry declined to comment on anonymous-source reporting. Lithuania and Latvia have not commented.

Critical take. The Estonian answers do not fully line up: the foreign ministry denies any official request, while the prime minister says allies are asking Estonia to hold back. Both can be true if the pressure was informal, which is what Reuters described. Lithuanian and Latvian silence is notable.

One network linked to Starlink arson attacks in Lithuania, Bulgaria and Poland

Official statement, on the record Lithuanian, Polish and Turkish authorities; LRT, The Insider, Turkish Minute and others.

Lithuanian police said on 29 October that two men in their early 20s, dual German citizens living in Germany, are suspected of forming a terrorist group and of the 18 October arson attempt at a Vilnius data centre storing Starlink equipment. They were detained in Germany on a Bulgarian warrant over a 9 October fire near Sofia. Police say the 23 September attack on a Starlink station in Poland is linked to them. Turkey detained 85 people on 27 October in raids on a crime gang suspected in the Polish attack.

Critical take. The cross-border link is now official. The Russian link is stated by Poland's intelligence minister and Lithuania's prime minister, and press reports say the gang's alleged leader lives under Russian protection; Lithuanian police say the motive is not yet established. The targets support Ukraine's communications, which fits the pattern of cheap proxy sabotage seen elsewhere.

Region-wide blackout in Kaliningrad overnight

Official statement, on the record Russian regional authorities; Kyiv Independent and UNN say the cause could not be verified.

Around midnight on 31 October power went out in Kaliningrad and towns including Gvardeysk, Svetlogorsk and Bagrationovsk, and mobile internet was disrupted. The governor's press secretary said emergency automation at the CHP-2 power plant shut down part of the equipment and the load was redistributed; power is being restored in stages.

Critical take. Nothing so far contradicts the technical explanation, and there is no sign of an attack or of military activity. Headlines saying the cause is unknown run ahead of the evidence in the other direction. Worth watching only if outages repeat.

Report: US presses Lithuania to let Belarusian potash through Klaipėda

Anonymous sources One original report (Politico), relayed by LRT; Lithuanian responses on the record.

Politico, citing four European officials, reports that US special envoy to Belarus John Coale has asked Lithuanian diplomats about easing restrictions on Belarusian potash, which EU sanctions bar from EU ports. Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys said the sanctions ban both imports and transit; national security adviser Deividas Matulionis warned of legal and financial consequences.

Critical take. Lithuania's refusal is on the record; the US request itself is not. Together with the Pentagon story, it suggests Washington is leaning on Baltic positions as it seeks a deal involving Minsk and Moscow. The State Department did not respond.

Putin: ready for war with Europe but does not want one

Official statement, on the record Kremlin remarks reported by TASS, the Telegraph, Kyiv Post and Bloomberg.

On 29 October, after a call initiated by Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian PM Jonas Gahr Støre, Putin said Russia does not want war with Europe, that such a war would have no winners, and that Russia is ready if others prepare for one. Danish PM Mette Frederiksen was sceptical of the call. The FT reported Putin spent much of it on history; Støre disputes that.

Critical take. A familiar line, repeated from December 2025: reassurance paired with a warning. It does not change the evidence picture, which shows continuing hybrid pressure. The disagreement between the FT and Støre about what was said on the call is unresolved.

Telegraph: Moscow may demand NATO pullback to 1997 lines

Anonymous sources One original report (Telegraph), widely repeated by Ukrainian and other outlets.

The Telegraph, citing unnamed diplomatic sources, reported that Russia is expected to seek a reduction of NATO's military presence in Eastern Europe to 1997 levels in peace talks, which could mean allied troops leaving Poland, the Baltic states and Finland. A European official said the position is the same as in January 2022. Russia has agreed to US-mediated talks with Ukraine in Abu Dhabi next month.

Critical take. Not confirmed by any official. It is consistent with Russia's December 2021 demands, which makes it plausible, but it has not been formally announced. Note the tension with the Kremlin's 29 October statement ruling out European participation in the Abu Dhabi round, while the Telegraph says Moscow wants Europeans at the table.

Lithuania to introduce universal conscription in 2032

Official statement, on the record LRT and BNS.

After a State Defence Council meeting on 29 October, President Gitanas Nausėda said universal conscription will start in 2032, raising the annual intake from over 5,000 to about 7,500. The government wanted it sooner, but the military said it needs time to train instructors and build housing.

Critical take. A long-term resilience measure, not a reaction to an imminent threat. The gap between the government's preferred pace and the army's readiness is worth noting.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified. Troop and logistics movements are only visible when someone publishes them, so a quiet indicator means nothing has been reported, not that nothing is happening. Not a substitute for official warnings from your government.
