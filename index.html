Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Russia offers NATO states 'non-aggression pacts'; Baltic and EU leaders call its security agenda a trap

Official statements Russian proposal reported by Militarnyi from a Moscow forum; European reactions reported by Euronews, LRT and UA.NEWS (citing the FT); Lithuania's foreign minister said the same on 10 November.

Russia's chief negotiator says a new European security system was the central topic in Abu Dhabi, and Europeans describe Moscow's demands as limits on NATO enlargement and exercises and a pull-back of forces from the eastern flank. On 12 November Alexei Drobinin, head of foreign-policy planning at Russia's foreign ministry, proposed bilateral agreements with individual NATO members that he said amount to mutual non-aggression pacts. At the Dublin summit Estonia's prime minister Kristen Michal called the talk of security architecture a trap, Lithuania's president Gitanas Nausėda said there must be no spheres of influence and NATO forces must not leave the east, and EU foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas said the subject can come only after a ceasefire and a peace deal.

Critical take. This is the part of the Ukraine talks that could directly change Baltic security. Separate pacts with individual members would weaken NATO's collective defence logic, and the Baltic states have the most to lose from any cap on allied forces. For now the US has not endorsed Moscow's agenda and European capitals reject it.

After meeting Putin, US envoys say Europeans will join the next round; the Kremlin does not confirm

Official statements US account reported by Meduza, TASS and Reuters; the Kremlin side by Reuters; Kyiv Independent confirms the Dublin meeting.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow on 12 November and then Zelensky near Dublin. Witkoff said progress was made, a new trilateral meeting has been set and European representatives will take part. Reuters reports that Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov did not confirm European participation and called Europe's stance a recipe for endless war; Putin said Europe must decide whether it wants peace or war and that Western troops in Ukraine after a deal would lead Russia to resume the war. No date for the next round was given.

Critical take. The two accounts do not match, so European participation should be treated as a US expectation, not an agreement. Putin's line on Western troops is aimed at the European 'coalition of the willing', in which the Baltic states take part.

Finland's military says it detected a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland

Official statement Finnish statement via Reuters and the Moscow Times; Ukrainian claim via Militarnyi; no imagery of the damage published.

A Finnish Defence Forces spokesperson told Reuters that the military detected a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland area on 11 November, is monitoring the situation and sees no immediate military threat to Finland. Ukraine's SBU says it hit the Buyan-M small missile ship Naro-Fominsk, which can carry Kalibr cruise missiles; analysts place the attack near Gogland Island. Estonian media say the ship returned to St Petersburg. Russia has not commented.

Critical take. An independent NATO-country confirmation that a strike took place makes Ukraine's claim much more credible, though the damage is unknown. It shows the war now reaches into the eastern Baltic Sea, close to Finnish and Estonian waters, which raises the risk of stray drones and incidents.

FSB says it broke up a Polish spy network in Kaliningrad; Poland calls it disinformation

Official statements (both sides); analysis Reported by Notes from Poland; the FSB material is the only evidence for the claim.

On 11 November, Poland's Independence Day, the FSB said it had detained two people it described as agents of Poland's military intelligence and internal security agency in Kaliningrad, and warned seven Polish and three Russian citizens. It published a video. A spokesman for Poland's security services, Jacek Dobrzyński, called it a provocation and an act of disinformation, and the foreign ministry urged extreme caution. Polish fact-checkers at OKO.press noted unnatural Polish wording in the published messages and summer foliage in the footage.

Critical take. Neither side's account is independently confirmed. A former Polish security spokesman warns Moscow may use the claim to support its earlier accusation that NATO is preparing to isolate Kaliningrad, a narrative Russia has paired with nuclear warnings since September.

Poland indicts a man accused of helping Russia sabotage a railway in 2025

Official statement Single official source, reported by Notes from Poland.

Poland's National Prosecutor's Office said on 12 November it has filed an indictment against a Ukrainian man accused of espionage and sabotage for Russian intelligence. Prosecutors say he scouted the line, chose where explosives would be placed and installed recording devices for the November 2025 attack on the Warsaw–Lublin railway, in which a C4 charge was detonated near Mika. The two main suspects fled to Belarus and are wanted by Interpol.

Critical take. A court has not yet tested the charges. The case fits the pattern in the Lithuanian assessment of Russian services recruiting foreign proxies for disruptive operations, and of Belarus serving as a safe haven.

RAND Europe sketches a 2030 Russian attack on Estonia, as a scenario, not a forecast

Analysis Single think-tank report, reported by Meduza.

Meduza reports a RAND Europe study that imagines Putin's death in 2027, a new hard-line leader, a frozen war in Ukraine, and a limited Russian operation in north-east Estonia in 2030 using protests in Narva as a pretext, with NATO invoking Article 5. The authors stress it is one possible path, not a prediction, but say a war in Europe by 2030 is a real risk.

Critical take. Useful as a thinking tool and consistent with the Lithuanian baseline's longer-term warning about limited operations against NATO states, but it is not intelligence and should not be read as evidence of current Russian plans.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified. Troop and logistics movements are only visible when someone publishes them, so a quiet indicator means nothing has been reported, not that nothing is happening. Not a substitute for official warnings from your government.
