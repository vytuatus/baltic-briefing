Critical assessment

What the evidence supports, and where the coverage runs ahead of it.

Today's picture is mostly words rather than events. Russia's OSCE envoy and the Kremlin spokesman sharpened their language, linking NATO drills near Kaliningrad and Ukrainian strikes on Russian ships in the Baltic to the risk of nuclear war, but neither made an explicit threat and Lithuania describes the drills as routine readiness training. The two drone alerts of 27 November came during a Ukrainian attack on the Leningrad region and neither Latvia nor Estonia has attributed them, so they should not be read as Russian probes until officials say so. The IISS study is useful context, showing more and more violent sabotage, but it covers a past period and adds no new case. A Telegraph report that a Russian warship jammed GPS on about 140 UK flights rests on an analysis that itself neither identifies nor rules out the ship, and concerns the North Sea, not the Baltic. Overall the evidence is consistent with the Lithuanian baseline: high hybrid pressure, hostile rhetoric and no sign of Russian forces being freed from Ukraine or redirected toward the Baltic states. Quiet logistics and medical indicators reflect a lack of public reporting, not proof that nothing is happening.

Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Estonia issues drone alert; Latvia reinforces its eastern air defence

Official statement ERR via LB.ua; Euronews citing the Latvian armed forces, LSM and AFP; Bytes Europe citing LRT.

Estonia's Defence Forces sent an air-threat alert to six southern and south-eastern counties at 06:40 on 27 November and lifted it at 07:24; NATO fighters were scrambled. The alert came hours after an unidentified drone briefly entered Latvia over Ludza, while Russia's Leningrad region governor reported a large drone attack there. Latvia's armed forces have sent extra air defence units to the eastern border and warned that more foreign drones could enter.

Critical take. Both alerts fit the pattern of Ukrainian long-range drones flying near the Baltic borders, sometimes diverted by Russian jamming. Neither country has attributed them, and no damage was reported.

Russia's OSCE envoy: NATO bringing the world 'closer to nuclear war'

Official statement TASS; Newsweek, which notes Lithuania calls the drills readiness training.

Speaking in Vienna on 26 November, Russia's permanent representative to the OSCE, Dmitry Polyansky, said NATO military activity near Russia's borders and the involvement of some NATO countries in attacks on Russian civilian ships in the Baltic bring the world closer to nuclear war. He claimed allied drills near Kaliningrad aim to rehearse seizing the region and cited the drone strike on the cargo ship Sparta II.

Critical take. A familiar Russian line, now tied to the Ukrainian strikes on Russian shipping. No evidence is offered that NATO states took part in those strikes, and the drills are publicly announced.

Kremlin: Ukraine is spreading the war to the Baltic

Official statement An aggregated summary of Reuters and Politico reports; the original articles could not be read for this edition.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on 26 November that Ukraine is trying to spread the war to the Baltic and Mediterranean seas, calling it dangerous. He was responding to a joint statement after US President Donald Trump met NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte, which urged Russia to avoid widening the war; Trump later said both Russia and Ukraine must avoid such actions.

Critical take. The exchange shows the Baltic Sea becoming a theatre of the Russia–Ukraine war through ship strikes, which raises the risk of incidents near Baltic and Finnish waters even without any plan to attack NATO.

IISS: Russia-linked sabotage in Europe up 16% in a year

Analysis LB.ua; Kyiv Independent via Yahoo.

A study by the International Institute for Strategic Studies, published on 26 November, counts a 16% rise in Russia-linked sabotage incidents in Europe from October 2025 to September 2026. Poland accounted for about a quarter of incidents, and Germany and the Baltic states for about a fifth each; arson and bomb attacks roughly doubled and five attempted assassinations were recorded. The study says the campaign has not achieved a strategic effect but warns a mass-casualty incident is becoming more possible.

Critical take. An independent count that supports the Lithuanian baseline's description of hybrid threats. It covers a past period, so it does not change today's counts.

Report: Russian warship suspected of jamming GPS on UK flights

Analysis The Telegraph via Yahoo; Anadolu Agency citing British media.

The Telegraph reported on 24 November, citing analysis by the aviation intelligence group Hexbird first published by the i paper, that a GPS outage on 9 November affected about 140 flights, with a likely source over the North Sea where a Russian warship was escorting sanctioned cargo ships. Hexbird said its analysis neither identifies nor rules out the convoy. Anadolu quotes an unnamed senior British defence source as sceptical; the UK government did not comment.

Critical take. The headlines run ahead of the evidence: the source itself does not attribute the outage. It also lies outside the Baltic region, so it is not counted here.

Lithuania: at least 5% of GDP on defence may be needed until 2035–2040

Official statement LRT/BNS.

Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas said on 27 November that Lithuania will need at least 5% of GDP for defence for a decade or longer, possibly 5.5%, to build a national division and strengthen air, maritime and border defence. The government has proposed 5% of GDP, over €4.8 billion, for next year.

Critical take. A long-term planning signal consistent with the baseline's view that the threat is about Russia's rebuilt capacity over years, not an imminent attack.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified. Troop and logistics movements are only visible when someone publishes them, so a quiet indicator means nothing has been reported, not that nothing is happening. Not a substitute for official warnings from your government.
