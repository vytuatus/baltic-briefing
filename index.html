Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Finnish court jails two officers of the Fitburg over Gulf of Finland cable damage

Official statement, on the record Euronews report on the court ruling. The article does not say whether the verdict will be appealed, and no Russian reaction was reported.

On 5 November the Helsinki District Court found the Russian captain of the cargo ship Fitburg and a senior crew member from Azerbaijan guilty of aggravated criminal damage, sentencing them to three years and six months and two years and ten months respectively. The St Vincent and the Grenadines-flagged ship, sailing from St Petersburg to Haifa, was detained on New Year's Eve; investigators say it dragged its anchor for several kilometres across undersea cables linking Finland and Estonia, including one owned by Elisa. Finnish customs had earlier found its steel cargo was under Russian sanctions.

Critical take. The first conviction in the recent run of Baltic cable cases, and a direct match for the baseline's warning about shadow-fleet incidents involving undersea infrastructure. Note what the court did and did not find: criminal damage by the crew, not a proven order from the Russian state. In October a separate Finnish court dismissed charges in the Eagle S case because intent could not be proved, so outcomes vary.

Russian satellites move toward a second Finnish radar satellite

Imagery or documents Reuters, carried by The Moscow Times and others (one wire story); orbital-tracking firms COMSPOC and Slingshot Aerospace; earlier coverage by Meduza and Militarnyi. Reported closest distances differ widely, from 26 metres (Militarnyi, using a satellite-map service) to about a kilometre (Reuters).

Reuters reported on 5 November that, after the Russian military satellite Cosmos 2612 came very close to ICEYE's X36 radar satellite in mid-October, two sibling Russian satellites are now drifting toward the orbit of another ICEYE satellite, X37. ICEYE, a Finnish company, supplies radar imagery to Western governments and to Ukraine. A US space analyst said the October approach looked like an attack rehearsal; the US Under Secretary of State for arms control, Thomas DiNanno, called it consistent with on-orbit weapons operations; Germany's space commander, Maj. Gen. Michael Traut, said such approaches can serve inspection or interference and are not by themselves proof of an attack plan. Russia's Defence Ministry did not comment.

Critical take. A real and unusual pattern that touches the Nordic-Baltic region directly, since radar satellites can see Russian movements at night and through cloud. But public orbital data cannot show intent, and the Robert Lansing Institute's broader conclusions are commentary. Watch for an official Finnish or NATO statement.

NATO begins six weeks of German-led drills in Lithuania

Official statement, on the record LRT and Militarnyi, citing the Lithuanian army; Kyiv Post on the Polish exercise.

Two German-led exercises, Freedom Shield 26-II at Rūdninkai and Grand Eagle 26 at the Pabradė training area, began on 6 November and run until 3 December with more than 6,000 troops from Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, the Czech Republic, Croatia, Belgium, Luxembourg and Lithuania. The army warned of convoy traffic from the Kalvarija checkpoint on 6–7 November. Separately, Poland's foreign minister said about 2,000 Polish troops will join the KOALICJA 26.2 exercise from 9 to 22 November.

Critical take. Routine but large defensive training. It is not a threat indicator, but the timing, during Poland's Kaliningrad alert, means Russian state media may present it as provocation, as it did with earlier drills. Readers should expect more military traffic on Lithuanian roads.

Mixed US signals for Lithuania: more troops, but a reported push on Belarus transit

Official statement, on the record LRT and BNS for the troop plans; Politico report relayed by LRT, with Lithuanian responses. The Pentagon's Europe posture review has not announced a decision.

Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas said after meeting the US ambassador to NATO at the Berlin Security Conference that Lithuania is ready to host additional US forces, and that two US battalions with equipment will be based at Pabradė, about 10 km from Belarus. At the same time Politico reported that US officials had raised with Lithuanian diplomats letting Belarusian fertiliser transit through the port of Klaipėda; a presidential adviser called the report outdated, while Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys denied that any such talks are happening.

Critical take. More US troops near the Suwałki corridor would strengthen deterrence, in line with what the baseline says is needed. The fertiliser report, if accurate, would show Washington testing a thaw with Minsk that Vilnius opposes; the denials do not fully match each other. No change to the threat picture yet.

Poland insists on 'ceasefire first' and a European seat ahead of Abu Dhabi talks

Official statement, on the record Kyiv Post (Sikorski); RFE/RL on the talks, citing US envoys and analysts.

Speaking in Warsaw on 6 November, Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said the order must be a ceasefire first, then talks on lasting peace, and only after that discussion of Europe's security architecture, which cannot happen without Europe and the eastern-flank states. He called for ending Schengen tourist visas for Russians and tougher action on the shadow fleet. The US is hosting Russia–Ukraine working-level talks in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November; analysts expect no breakthrough.

Critical take. The eastern-flank concern is that a US–Russia framework on European security could be agreed over their heads. A ceasefire could in time free Russian forces, the main way the baseline could shift; nothing so far suggests that is imminent.

ACLED: 20 suspected Russian hybrid events in Poland this year

Analysis ACLED data via Newsweek; consistent with Polish officials' statements that nearly 100 people have been charged in espionage and sabotage cases since January 2024.

Newsweek, citing the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data project (ACLED), reported on 6 November that it has recorded 20 suspected Russian hybrid events in Poland in 2026, including sabotage, intrusions, drone surveillance and arson, half of them against transport or defence infrastructure. ACLED's analyst Maksym Beznosiuk expects more such activity in Poland over the next six months, especially in border regions.

Critical take. Useful independent counting, but 'suspected' is the key word: many cases are attributed to Russia by officials before any court ruling. The trend, not each case, is the reliable signal.

How this briefing works

Source priority

  • Official and intelligence: Baltic, Nordic, German, UK and US services, NATO and defence ministries.
  • Quality journalism: Baltic broadcasters, Ukrainian outlets, Reuters, AP and major international papers.
  • Analysts and open-source investigators: ISW, RUSI, satellite-imagery analysts and incident trackers.
  • The Russian side: independent Russian outlets, plus state media to see the Kremlin's narrative.
  • Commentators and influencers: included, always labelled as commentary rather than evidence.

Evidence labels

  • Official statement: on the record, attributable.
  • Imagery or documents: something you can inspect.
  • Anonymous sources: credible outlet, not verifiable.
  • Unverified claim: a single or partisan source.

Many outlets repeating one wire report is one source, not many.

Limits

Built from the public web only: no dark-web, classified or paywalled material. Intelligence claims are reported as stated, not independently verified. Troop and logistics movements are only visible when someone publishes them, so a quiet indicator means nothing has been reported, not that nothing is happening. Not a substitute for official warnings from your government.
