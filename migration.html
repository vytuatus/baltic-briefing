Critical assessment

What the evidence supports, and where the coverage runs ahead of it.

Organised pressure from Belarus continues, and Latvia's year total now exceeds all of 2025, which supports the Elevated rating; but the totals count attempts, the same person may be counted several times, and no new daily figures appeared in the last two days, so no short-term trend can be drawn. On the EU side nothing new points to other states shifting migrants onto the Baltic states: the Commission's designations give all three priority access to support, and the main open question is the 2027 pool the Council still has to set. The only regular transfers involving the Baltic states found this week are lawful returns from Estonia to Latvia of people who moved north after crossing from Belarus, which is a consequence of the Belarus route, not a burden shifted from western Europe. Lithuania's Kaliningrad fence plan is aimed mainly at hybrid threats and possible Russian draft evaders, not at a current migration flow. Coverage that treats Lithuania's or Estonia's pool status as settled runs ahead of the decision, which has not been taken.

Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Latvia's prevented crossings pass 13,000 for the year

Official statement Border guard figures carried by LETA, Diena and Reform.news; counted once.

Latvia's State Border Guard reported 29 prevented attempts on 16 November, bringing the 2026 total to about 13,012, more than the 12,046 for all of 2025 and close to the 2023 record of 13,863. Another 6 attempts followed on 17 November. Latvia has extended its reinforced border regime in five municipalities until the end of the year.

Critical take. The record is a measure of attempts, not people, and officials acknowledge repeat attempts; it shows sustained organised pressure on Latvia rather than a new surge.

Baltic states' status in the EU solidarity system: 'at risk', not exempt

Official statement Reported by Bernama and VisaVerge from the Commission's annual report.

The Commission's 6 November assessment named Cyprus, Greece, Italy and Spain as under migratory pressure and eligible for the 2027 solidarity pool. It listed 15 states as at risk, including Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia, giving them priority access to EU support, and six states, including Estonia and Poland, as facing a significant migratory situation, which lets them ask the Council to reduce their contributions. The Council is expected to set the pool's size and each state's share before the end of the year.

Critical take. Reports that Poland and Estonia are 'exempt' overstate the position: they may request deductions, which the Council must still decide. Nothing in the designations obliges any Baltic state to take relocated migrants.

Lithuania plans a fence on parts of the Kaliningrad border

Official statement Reported by LRT and European Pravda.

Lithuania's border guard service has drawn up a plan to reinforce the border with Kaliningrad, including a segmented barbed-wire fence on selected stretches. The interior minister said more money is needed and linked army help at the border to a possible Russian mobilisation after the Duma elections.

Critical take. The plan is mainly about hybrid threats and possible draft evaders from Russia, not a current migration flow; officials say the threat level has not risen.

Estonia has returned over 400 people to Latvia this year

Official statement Police and prosecution figures reported by ERR.

ERR reported that more than 400 migrants caught in Estonia this year after travelling from Latvia were returned there, and that 11 people have been detained on suspicion of smuggling. A court sentenced a 24-year-old foreign man to a year in prison for driving five migrants from Latvia to Estonia.

Critical take. These are lawful returns between neighbouring states for people who entered via Belarus, not western European states shifting migrants onto the Baltic states.

How the migration tab works

What it tracks

  • Belarus and Russia: crossing attempts, smuggling networks and organised pressure at the eastern border.
  • EU and Schengen: EU solidarity and relocation decisions, transfers of migrants back to the Baltic states, and border checks inside Schengen.
  • Domestic: asylum claims, reception capacity, laws and court rulings.
  • Information: false or misleading claims about migration.

Sources and balance

Border guards, interior ministries, prosecutors, the European Commission, the Council and the EU asylum agency come first, then Baltic, Polish and international media, fact-checkers and rights groups. Government figures and rights groups' concerns are both reported, so readers can weigh them.

Limits

Crossing figures count prevented attempts, not people, and are published unevenly from day to day. Lawful returns between EU countries are kept apart from pressure organised by Belarus or Russia. Not legal advice.
