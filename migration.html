Critical assessment

What the evidence supports, and where the coverage runs ahead of it.

The Latvian lull has now lasted four days, the longest in weeks, but it comes from one unevenly published series and earlier pauses this year were followed by new peaks, so reports of a calm border run ahead of the evidence. Poland's decision to renew its buffer zone shows that at least one government still sees real pressure from Belarus. On the user's main concern, nothing suggests other EU states are shifting migrants onto the Baltic states: the only arrivals from another EU country are the 58 people from Cyprus that Lithuania itself agreed to take, and Lithuania now plans to pay rather than relocate in 2027, a choice the Migration Pact allows. That choice is politically contested at home, with opposition figures criticising it and an MP claiming without evidence that Cyprus sent particularly problematic cases. Lawful returns between neighbours, such as Estonia's to Latvia, continue under EU rules and are separate from pressure organised by Belarus or Russia.

Key reporting

The stories that matter today, each with the type of evidence behind it and a critical take.

Latvia: four days in a row without attempts from Belarus

Official statement LETA via 1188.lv and Diena; the border guard's own page could not be retrieved, so the exact dates are inferred.

Latvia's State Border Guard reported on 27 November that it had recorded no attempts by migrants to cross from Belarus for four days in a row, after reporting three such days on 26 November. Mid-November figures had been in single digits, after 20 to 40 attempts a day earlier in the month. This year's total, about 13,035, remains above the 12,046 recorded in all of 2025.

Critical take. The longest lull in weeks, but a single series that counts attempts rather than people. It does not show that Belarus has stopped organising crossings.

Poland renews its Belarus-border buffer zone for 90 days

Official statement Polsat News citing PAP.

Poland has extended the buffer zone along its border with Belarus for December, January and February, the tenth extension since June 2024, citing still real threats linked to migratory pressure. PAP reports 482 attempted illegal crossings on the Polish–Belarusian section from January to 8 November, and 13 fines and 141 warnings issued for presence in the zone by the Podlasie border guard by late October. The zone's area is unchanged.

Critical take. A routine renewal rather than an escalation, but it shows Warsaw does not regard the pressure as over.

Lithuania plans to pay up to €4 million instead of taking relocated asylum seekers

Official statement LRT; BNN rewrite of the same report.

Interior Minister Martynas Katelynas plans that Lithuania accept no relocated asylum seekers in 2027 and instead pay into the EU solidarity pool, possibly up to €4 million. This year Lithuania agreed to take 58 people from Cyprus: 28 have arrived, another group is due in December, and it has paid nearly €1.5 million for the remainder. Opposition figures criticised the plan, while the Seimas foreign and European affairs committees supported it; an MP claimed Cyprus had sent particularly problematic cases.

Critical take. Paying instead of relocating is one of the options the Migration Pact offers every state, so this is a lawful choice, not a sign of pressure from other EU countries.

Estonia closes Russia road crossings on 1 December; Latvia to decide next week

Official statement Bytes Europe citing Delfi.

Estonia's road crossings with Russia close on 1 December for at least six months. Latvia's prime minister has asked ministries to assess restrictions on security, transport and goods transit, with the issue expected in the week of 30 November; the interior minister backs discussing it but says the coalition needs a common position, and the border guard chief has warned that traffic will shift to Latvia.

Critical take. A security measure rather than a migration one, but uneven decisions could shift travellers, and any pressure, along the Baltic border with Russia.
